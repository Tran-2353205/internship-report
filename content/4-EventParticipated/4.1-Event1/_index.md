# Summary Report Report: "Saturday Meet up"

### Event Purpose

The event was organized to help students and young engineers gain a clearer understanding of real-world practices in Cloud, AI, DevOps, and Data Analytics. It also provided insights into recruitment processes, working cultures, and career development roadmaps at multinational corporations through direct sharing from experienced working engineers.

### List of Speakers

- **Trong H. Truong** - DevOps Engineer @ Endava Vietnam.
- **Danh Hoang Hieu Nghi** - AI Engineer, AWS Community Builder, AWS Student Builder Group Leader.
- **Mr. Dat Pham** - Data Analytics Engineer, former employee at Kamereo and Colgate-Palmolive.
- **Mr. Cuong Nguyen** - Process Engineer.

---

### Key Highlights

#### 1. Data Analytics Engineer - Real-World Responsibilities

This was the section I paid the most attention to, as it clearly showed that the work of a Data Analytics Engineer is not just "making reports and drawing charts" as many people assume.

Speaker Dat Pham shared that daily responsibilities vary depending on the business context: at a logistics company like Kamereo, it involves building operational tracking reports, developing anomaly detection dashboards, and conducting root-cause analysis; whereas at a manufacturing enterprise like Colgate-Palmolive, it revolves around IoT data, manufacturing cost optimization, and supporting digital transformation. From this, I realized that for the same job title, the core tasks differ based on each company's specific business problems.

A Data Analytics Engineer needs 4 core skills: **Critical Thinking** (not just reporting metrics, but identifying why they fluctuate), **Communication** (conveying insights clearly to any audience), **Data Storytelling** (transforming dry numbers into compelling narratives to aid decision-making), and **Problem-Solving**.

My favorite part was the 5-level career development model that the speaker used for self-orientation, focusing on capability growth rather than titles: **Follower** (following instructions) → **Learner** (proactively learning and asking deep questions) → **Problem Solver** (proactively analyzing and proposing solutions) → **System Thinker** (viewing the system holistically and predicting risks) → **Super Star** (setting strategic direction and guiding the next generation). I find this model highly applicable even for students like me, who are currently floating around the Follower/Learner stage.

---

#### 2. Corporate Culture in Multinational Corporations (MNCs)

This section gave me the most valuable insights, as I previously thought "company culture" was simply team-building activities, without realizing how deeply it impacts how an organization operates and makes decisions.

The speaker outlined a standard 4-step recruitment process at MNCs: **Screening & Initial Interview** (ATS resume scanning, quick English screening) → **Competency Assessment** (logic/algorithm tests or Situation Tests) → **Technical Interview** (with Tech Lead/Manager using the STAR method) → **Cultural Fit** (leadership interview).

The speaker quoted Dr. Gian Tu Trung (author of *Right Work*): corporate culture is essentially the mindset, lifestyle, and working style of every individual within the organization—not just slogans on the wall. Two examples that impressed me the most were:

- **No-Blame Post-Mortem** (Tech MNCs): When a system outage occurs, the focus is on finding and fixing the root cause rather than assigning personal blame, encouraging the team to report issues early instead of hiding them.
- **Caring & Inclusive** (FMCG MNCs): Placing people at the center, respecting diversity, and continuously improving.

The speaker expanded to a broader macroeconomic perspective with East Asian lessons: Japan with the "Wakon Yosai" spirit (preserving cultural identity while mastering Western technology, epitomized by Toyota's TPS system) and South Korea with the "Miracle on the Han River" (building Chaebols compelled to meet strict international standards). Through this, I understood that the culture and working mindset of a nation or organization mature gradually over time, rather than emerging overnight.

---

#### 3. DevOps - Real-World Role (Overview)

Speaker Trong H. Truong briefly shared: DevOps is not just about "writing CI/CD pipelines" or "tweaking Docker/Kubernetes," but about solving daily system operation challenges. Essential foundational knowledge includes Linux, Networking, Python/Golang, Git, CI/CD, and Containers. Key takeaway: copying commands doesn't mean understanding them; ask "why" before asking "how".

#### 4. From Student to AWS Partner (Overview)

Speaker Danh Hoang Hieu Nghi briefly shared his journey from a tech-curious student, participating in programs like First Cloud AI Journey and AWS Student Builder Group, to becoming an AWS Community Builder and working with an AWS Partner. Key message: *"Getting the job is just a beginning"*.

### Key Learnings

- Gained a deeper understanding of real-world responsibilities and the 4 core skills of a Data Analytics Engineer.
- Understood that corporate culture is expressed through actions (No-Blame, Caring & Inclusive) rather than slogans.
- Learned about the standard MNC recruitment process.
- Acquired a general overview of DevOps roles and the Cloud/AI career path for students.

### Practical Application

- Use the 5-level growth model (Follower → Super Star) to self-assess my current capabilities.
- When creating reports or working on team projects, build the habit of analyzing root causes and proposing solutions instead of just presenting raw data.
- Apply the No-Blame mindset during teamwork: fix processes rather than blaming individuals.

### Event Experience

Attending the event provided me with practical perspectives, especially in the two areas I am most interested in: Data Analytics and corporate culture—topics I had not previously explored in depth. I realized that working in a business environment requires not only technical proficiency but also an understanding of organizational culture and professional problem-solving.

#### Lessons Learned

- Corporate culture directly impacts whether a team feels safe to acknowledge mistakes and continuously improve.
- Data Analytics is not just about tools; it relies heavily on critical thinking and the ability to tell stories with data.
- Career development is a multi-stage journey that requires patience rather than chasing titles prematurely.
